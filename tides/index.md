The tide at a place is the sum of a few dozen steady waves, each driven by the moon or the sun. **Harmonic constants** give each wave's size (amplitude) and timing (phase) at one station, measured from a year or more of water levels. To predict, each wave is started from where the moon and sun are at the time, adjusted for the 18.6-year wobble of the moon's orbit, and the waves are added up. NOAA predicts every US tide this way, with the method in Schureman's manual from 1940.
